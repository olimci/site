# Aesthetic field notes

This is not a brand workshop and there are no correct answers. It is a way to
find the visual instincts that should govern the site, especially the home-page
background.

Answer quickly and concretely. A half-sentence, a link, an image, a colour, or
"I hate this question" can all be useful answers. Contradictions are useful too.
Do not try to sound consistent or tasteful.

You do not need to answer everything. Start with the **short pass**. The rest is
there when a question catches your interest.

When an answer depends on an example, add the example rather than trying to
describe it perfectly. Screenshots and badly cropped phone photos are welcome.

## Short pass

### 1. What is directionally right about the current hero?

Is it the darkness, scale, full-screen pause, movement, iridescence, custom
lettering, lack of copy, sense of depth, pointer response, or something harder
to name?

**Answer:**

Hard to say- It's absolutely the full-screen-ness- it goes very well with the browser shape etc. The movement is important too I think, I'm not sure a static screen would really carry the same weight iykwim. I'm very much into things being "illegible" (i'll explain more about this somewhere else) at first glance, so the lack of copy is definitely important to me.

### 2. What feels wrong about it?

Describe the first thing that bothers you before explaining why.

**Answer:**

The colours, I'm not big on, it's very washed-out, and the blue/pink behind that doesn't really feel like my personal brand. Also the glow around the cursor feels very directionless.

It also feels almost _too organic_ for lack of a better description. The wobbliness (esp cursor reactivity doesn't help it much I feel.). Though please don't take this as "I don't like organic things," I do, but I think a specific axis of organic-ness is what I like.

### 3. What three words should the first five seconds of the site feel like?

Avoid words that merely sound complimentary. "Cold", "severe", "odd", and
"quiet" are more useful than "beautiful" or "professional".

**Answer:**

Cold is very much the feeling. But I don't think coldness-as-in-temperature. Like I feel coldness as in "coldness be my god,"- kind of as in uncaring. The sort of coldness that brutalist architecture evokes. Like cold in the way that BLAME! feels cold.

Illegibility is also an inspiration I would want to draw from. But It's hard to disentangle from symbols, like something can be both typographic, and illegible. I want it to feel purposeful, but I want the purpose to be opaque, I guess is the best way to say that. Atypography is a great example of what I mean I think.

I think think also, architectural / brutalist. I really like the feeling of "fear of god", that cathedrals, or large-scale engineering projects, like dams etc inspire.

So: Cold / Illegible / Brutalist maybe?

### 4. What should it definitely not feel like?

Name websites, industries, visual trends, or personality types if useful.

**Answer:**

I don't want it to feel LARPy (like I don't want it to be pretending to be anything.)

I'm pretty against any kind of corporate or professional look^1. Similarly I don't really like "quaint" things.

I'm not big on Hacker News, either. There's a specific way that Hacker News poster's websites feel and it kind of ticks me off hahahaha.

^1: though I really like corporate, as in old IBM, or these sort of "institutional" corporate designs. Complete form-over function, and a strong sense of weight. I think what I'm not into is the modern clean/friendly feeling.

## Shape, order, and composition

### 41. Are you more attracted to fields, objects, diagrams, patterns, or landscapes?

**Answer:**

This is a hard question- fields and diagrams are both maps, like an abstracted representation of some landscape. Objects are more concrete, while lacking abstract, while patterns are entirely abstract and have no physical representation, both are items of maps.

Landscapes are the truest of these, in that all of those derive their properties from some landscape. Not to say that they are in any way better. Most of my photography is landscape photography though (I guess you could argue pylon photos are of objects, and also patterns? though.)

I'm not sure which of these I prefer.

### 42. Do you prefer geometry that is visibly constructed or geometry that emerges from a process?

**Answer:**

Emergent geometry is definitely more interesting, but I think a lot of constructed geometry is actually emergent, like supports in buildings for example are definitely engineered, but are effectively emergent propertyies of the building's structure. Though purely constructed geometry can be a bit unaesthetically symmetric I think.

### 43. How do you feel about symmetry?

When is it satisfying, and when does it feel dead?

**Answer:**

Symmetry I feel is quite often bad. It's kind of a cheap getout of having an unaesethetic substance behind what you originally have. The best parts are assymetry in order (for example, take a randomly oriented hypercube projected into 2d, it is a symmetric, ordered object, which has little-to-no symmetry in its projection)

### 44. How orderly should the effect be?

Describe the point at which order becomes boring and chaos becomes meaningless.

**Answer:**

I would like it to feel ordered- but I don't want you to necessarily be able to predict the entirety of the effect once you have worked out the pattern.

### 45. Do you enjoy repetition when tiny differences accumulate?

What repeated things do you find compelling?

**Answer:**

I like order between scales. Like the orderedness exists as an intensive property of the substrate, rather than being a property of the scale of the viewer. Purely repeated patterns on one scale are a bit boring.

### 46. Which scale feels most interesting here?

Microscopic, human, architectural, geographic, planetary, astronomical, or a
scale that cannot be identified?

**Answer:**

I like architectural-geographic scale things personally.

### 47. Should the image imply a world beyond the edges of the screen, or should it feel like a complete poster fitted to the viewport?

**Answer:**

Yes I think that probably is the most interesting option. Though I wouldn't like to constrain your creativity.

### 48. Do you prefer a single striking event or a field full of small details?

**Answer:**

I think lots of small details. It's what gives something "replay value" really.

### 49. How much visual tension do you want?

Could something feel slightly off-centre, cropped, unstable, interrupted, or
about to happen?

**Answer:**

This can be good. It's easy to do wrong if you are trying to do this intentionally though.

### 50. What kinds of emptiness feel intentional to you?

**Answer:**

Structred emptiness. If something is missing, it should be missing for a reason.

## Practical boundaries

### 96. Who are you happiest to impress with the site?

Who do you not mind confusing or alienating?

**Answer:**

I don't mind alienating nontechnical people. Precedent says employers like the edgier parts of my site "ARP fuckery" etc, so I'm leaning more toward that.

### 97. Should the hero explain who you are, or earn enough curiosity that the visitor scrolls?

**Answer:**

I suppose we can earn curiosity through illegibility? If the hero is visually striking enough that it hooks people, we don't need to hook them with basically my CV you know?

### 98. How important is instant loading compared with visual richness?

What kind of delay or fallback would feel acceptable?

**Answer:**

Keeping it snappy is good, so no massive assets, but you would have to make an extremely heavy-weight shader to make it load slower than the avg nextjs or whatever site nowadays.

### 99. How much GPU and battery use feels reasonable for a personal-site hero?

**Answer:**

Don't need to try too hard, just don't make something that will make my phone warm hahaha.

### 100. Should it work as intended on older devices, or is a simpler still composition an honest fallback?

**Answer:**

Nowadays people don't really have many legacy browsers. Or at least there is a very small intersection between people with legacy browsers and people who I think would enjoy my site.

### 101. Would you accept an effect that changes somewhat across browsers and displays?

Which differences would feel like character, and which would feel broken?

**Answer:**

As long as it always looks nice, then that's fine. Can make it break on old devices though.

### 102. How often do you want to revisit the design?

Should this be a durable identity, an experiment you can replace freely, or a
system with room for seasonal mutations?

**Answer:**

I tend to update my site a lot. So I guess it's not worth making it load-bearing, but also if it's good enough, then it can become part of my identity. We can alternatively take the opposite, I'm known for liking pylon photos already so having those would make it relate to my identity for example.
